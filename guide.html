Educator guide

Early Learnings · Play, Discover, Grow

Early Learnings is a calm, offline set of activities for children aged 2 to 6. It is designed for short, guided sessions alongside an adult, not as a replacement for hands-on play. There are no accounts, scores, leaderboards, advertising or tracking.

Getting started

  1. Tap Settings (top right of the home screen). The first time, answer the grown-ups question and choose a 4-digit PIN.
  2. Choose the age group. Every activity adjusts: subjects, difficulty, number ranges, puzzle sizes and tracing.
  3. Turn on Calm Mode for a quiet room: no music or sound effects, no confetti, nothing bounces or flashes. The voice can stay on or be turned off separately.
  4. Optional: set a session length, hide activities you don't want today, or turn on group turns.
  5. Tap Done. Children only ever see the five big category cards and a home button.
Keeping children inside the app: on an iPad, turn on Guided Access (Settings › Accessibility › Guided Access), open Early Learnings and triple-click the Home or top button. The child cannot leave the app until you triple-click again and enter your passcode.

The five categories and what they build

Discover

Activities: Letters, Numbers, Words, First Words, Colours, Shapes, Our World, Flashcards.

Learning objectives: letter recognition and first sounds; counting, numerals and early adding and taking away; vocabulary; naming colours and shapes; early science ideas such as weather, living things and where food comes from.

Think

Activities: Patterns, Puzzles, Memory, Jigsaw, Maze, Feed & Sort.

Learning objectives: noticing and continuing patterns; comparing and ordering; matching and visual memory; sorting by one attribute; planning a route; telling time (older groups).

Create

Activities: Draw, Colouring, Write (tracing), Dot to Dot, Silly Faces, Music.

Learning objectives: fine-motor control and pencil paths; letter and number formation; counting in order (and by twos at 5–6); self-expression; cause and effect with sound.

Explore

Activities: I Spy, Animals, Hero Academy (nine short missions with Rory).

Learning objectives: observation and listening to instructions; animal names, sounds, homes and groups; counting, colours, shapes and sizes in context; kindness, feelings, manners, safety and problem solving.

Relax

Activities: Bubbles, Starry Sky.

Learning objectives: simple cause and effect; calming, open-ended play with no right or wrong answers. Useful for winding down or for children who need a low-demand activity.

What each age group practises

Age groupFocus
2–3First words and body parts, colours, simple shapes, counting to 3 and 5, big and small, matching, animal sounds, feelings, tracing lines.
3–4Letter names and first sounds, counting to 10 and numerals, rhymes, simple patterns, sorting and comparing, colour mixing, kindness and taking turns, tracing letters and numbers.
4–5All letters, first and last letters, reading short words, counting to 20, adding and taking away within 10, first-second-third, time to the hour, the world around us, writing short words.
5–6Reading and spelling longer words, adding and taking away within 20, skip counting, tens and ones, time to the half hour, halves, 3D shapes, measuring, early science, safety and problem solving.

Progress is kept separately for each age group, so one device can be shared between rooms.

Suggestions for group use

  • Keep sessions short. 10 to 15 minutes is plenty for most children under five. The session timer shows a gentle "all done" screen that only an educator can dismiss.
  • Group turns shows a coloured number, or the child’s first name if you enter it, after each question so two to four children can share one device fairly.
  • Talk about it. Ask "How did you know?" or "What do you think comes next?" The conversation matters more than the tap.
  • Take it off the screen. After a sorting game, sort real objects. After Starry Sky, draw stars with chalk. The printable sheets continue the same skills on paper.
  • Difficulty adjusts itself: a subject moves up after four correct answers in a row and eases off if a child struggles. You can set any level by hand in Settings › Difficulty.

What the app does not do

It does not track individual children, keep scores between children, show leaderboards, connect to the internet during play, or contain links that lead outside the app. Stars and levels are simply saved on the device so activities stay at the right difficulty.
